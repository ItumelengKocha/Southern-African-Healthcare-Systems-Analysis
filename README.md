# Southern African Healthcare Systems Analytics

## Comparative Study of South Africa, Lesotho and the Democratic Republic of the Congo

A comparative healthcare-systems research project exploring how differences in healthcare governance, structure, financing, workforce and service delivery influence health data collection, reporting and decision-making across Southern Africa.

## Project Overview

Healthcare data does not exist in isolation. Every patient record, laboratory result, vaccination register and disease notification is produced within a healthcare system shaped by governance, funding, workforce and service delivery.

This project compares the healthcare systems of:

- South Africa
- Lesotho
- Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC)

The purpose was to understand how differences in healthcare-system design affect the way health information is generated, reported and used for decision-making.

The project also explores how healthcare analytics can support disease surveillance, key performance indicators, reporting quality, resource allocation and evidence-based healthcare planning.

## Project Objectives

The project aimed to:

- Compare healthcare systems across South Africa, Lesotho and the DRC.
- Understand how healthcare structures influence data collection and reporting.
- Identify strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats within each system.
- Explore practical applications of healthcare analytics in Southern Africa.
- Recommend data-driven strategies that could support improved healthcare delivery.
- Build foundational knowledge for future healthcare analytics projects using DHIS2, SQL, Power BI and healthcare datasets.

## Why Southern Africa?

Southern Africa provides an opportunity to compare neighbouring countries that face many similar health challenges while operating under very different healthcare systems.

The project examined:

### South Africa

South Africa has a large and complex healthcare system divided between public and private sectors, with healthcare governance operating through national, provincial and district structures.

The country is also undergoing major health-system reform through the implementation of National Health Insurance (NHI).

### Lesotho

Lesotho's healthcare system has a strong community-based component and relies heavily on Village Health Workers and collaboration between government and faith-based healthcare providers to reach rural and mountainous communities.

### Democratic Republic of the Congo

The DRC operates a highly decentralized healthcare system based around Health Zones, allowing healthcare activities and disease-response efforts to be organized closer to local communities despite significant resource constraints.

Comparing these systems helped me understand how geography, economics, governance and healthcare-system design influence healthcare delivery and health information.

## Comparative Healthcare Systems Analysis

### South Africa

The analysis examined:

- Governance through the National Department of Health, provincial departments and districts.
- Public and private healthcare sectors.
- Primary, district, regional and central/tertiary levels of care.
- Community health worker programmes.
- Major health challenges including HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, non-communicable diseases and trauma.
- Healthcare financing through public-sector taxation, private medical schemes and targeted grants.
- Strengths including specialized tertiary infrastructure, chronic medicine delivery programmes and the National Health Laboratory Service.
- Challenges including pressure on public clinics, geographic inequalities in private healthcare and barriers to healthcare access and treatment retention.

A major observation from the analysis was the unequal distribution of healthcare resources between the public and private sectors.

## Lesotho

The analysis examined:

- Central oversight by the Ministry of Health.
- Healthcare delivery across 10 administrative districts.
- Collaboration between government and faith-based providers.
- Levels of care from Village Health Posts through Primary Health Centres and District Hospitals to Central Referral Hospitals.
- The role of Village Health Workers.
- Major health challenges including HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, maternal mortality and childhood malnutrition.
- Healthcare financing through government funding, donor support and subsidization of faith-based health services.
- Strengths in community-level healthcare access.
- Challenges created by mountainous terrain and barriers to physical access.

One of the major lessons from the analysis was the importance of Village Health Workers in extending healthcare services into remote communities.

## Democratic Republic of the Congo

The analysis examined:

- Decentralized healthcare governance.
- Provincial Health Divisions.
- The role of Health Zones as local operational units.
- Community-level healthcare delivery.
- General Referral Hospitals.
- The fragmented combination of public, humanitarian, faith-based and private providers.
- Major health challenges and resource constraints.
- The role of decentralized structures in responding to disease outbreaks.

The DRC provided an important example of how healthcare systems can use decentralized structures to respond to local health needs despite significant resource limitations.

## Recommendations

The project identified several practical areas where healthcare analytics and digital health could support stronger healthcare systems.

### Improve DHIS2 Reporting Completeness

Move beyond simply collecting data and identify facilities that are not reporting.

Simple reporting-completeness dashboards could help identify facilities with missing or delayed reports.

### Strengthen Community Health Worker Support

Basic digital tools, including offline-capable mobile forms, could reduce the burden of paper-based registers and improve the speed of data transmission for community health workers in rural areas.

### Improve Supply-Chain Monitoring

Supply-chain dashboards could connect facility stock information with regional medical depots to help identify potential medicine and vaccine stock-outs.

### Increase Interoperability

Healthcare systems could benefit from stronger connections between separate information systems so that relevant information can be shared more effectively between healthcare providers and public health structures.

### Expand Digital Health Infrastructure

Digital health initiatives in rural areas need to consider practical infrastructure requirements such as reliable electricity, device charging and connectivity.

## Limitations

This project is a comparative healthcare-systems research project rather than a quantitative analysis of national healthcare datasets.

The findings are therefore based on the sources reviewed for the report and should be interpreted within the scope of those sources.

Healthcare systems are complex and continue to change, particularly in areas such as digital health, National Health Insurance, disease surveillance and healthcare financing.

The project also provides a high-level comparison of three countries and cannot capture every regional, provincial, district or facility-level difference within each healthcare system.

The recommendations are practical analytics and digital-health opportunities identified through the research. They are not presented as formal government policy proposals.

## Conclusion

This project helped me understand that healthcare analytics begins before the data reaches a spreadsheet, database or dashboard.

The structure of a healthcare system influences how data is collected, who reports it, how complete it is and how confidently it can be used for decision-making.

Comparing South Africa, Lesotho and the Democratic Republic of the Congo helped me develop a stronger understanding of the relationship between healthcare systems and health information.

It also helped me identify practical ways that healthcare analytics, DHIS2, digital tools and better reporting could support healthcare decision-making in resource-constrained settings.
